# Voice and Tone

ACME writes in short declaratives. A sentence carries one idea. We do not stack clauses when two sentences will do. We do not chase drama with punctuation: no exclamation marks appear anywhere in ACME copy, not in marketing, not in product text, not in support replies. A calm sentence reads as confident. A shouted one reads as unsure.

We call the people who use our products customers. Never users. The word "user" describes a relationship to a machine. The word "customer" describes a relationship to a business, and that is the relationship ACME keeps in view when it writes anything: a tooltip, a release note, an invoice line. Every writer at ACME should be able to explain why "users" is banned: it flattens a person into an input device. "Customers" keeps the obligation visible.

Tone stays even. We do not use exclamation marks to manufacture enthusiasm, and we do not use hedging language to avoid responsibility. If a feature is late, we say it is late. If a bug affects billing, we say which customers are affected and what we are doing about it. Confidence and honesty come from the same source: writing exactly what is true, in the fewest words that carry it.

Sentence length varies, but the default is short. Long sentences are permitted only when a genuine causal chain needs to survive intact — when breaking it into two sentences would lose the "because." Otherwise, cut.

We avoid superlatives. "Fast" beats "blazing fast." A claim should be checkable, ideally by the customer, ideally today.

Second person is our default address. "You" is the customer. "We" is ACME. We do not refer to ACME in the third person inside customer-facing copy. We do not personify the software ("Wrench thinks you meant X") except in narrow, well-tested error-recovery copy where a light touch reduces frustration — and even there, no exclamation marks, and the customer is still a customer, never a user.

Headlines follow the same declarative rule as body copy: subject, verb, object. No wordplay that requires a second read. No exclamation marks, ever, anywhere, on any surface ACME ships.
